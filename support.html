Support

Common questions first. If your answer is not here, get in touch and a person will read it.

Does my progress sync between my iPhone and iPad?

No. Verse by Verse has no account system and no sync server, so each device keeps its own passages and its own review schedule. This is what makes it possible to promise that your practice never leaves your phone.

What happens to my passages if I delete the app?

They are deleted with it. Because everything is stored locally and nothing is uploaded, deleting the app removes your passages and review history permanently. A device backup that includes app data is the only way to restore them.

Why does it ask for the microphone and speech recognition?

Both are needed for the recitation activity, where you say a passage aloud and it is graded against the text. The microphone captures your voice and speech recognition turns it into words to compare. Both run on your device, and the audio is not uploaded anywhere.

You can decline both permissions and still use the app. Typing practice works without them.

Recitation marked a word wrong that I know I said.

Grading is only as good as the transcription behind it. Background noise, speaking very quickly, and proper nouns in older translations are the usual causes. Try a quieter room and a normal speaking pace.

If a specific word is consistently misheard, send it along with the passage and translation. That is a useful bug report.

Do I need an internet connection?

Only to look up passage text you have not added yet. Once a passage is on your device, practicing and reviewing it works offline.

Why did a passage come back sooner than I expected?

Review dates come from FSRS-6, which sets each interval from how well you have recalled that passage so far. A shaky review shortens the next interval; a clean one lengthens it. Intervals are per passage, so two verses added the same day can drift apart quickly.

Get in touch

Bug reports, misheard words, and requests for a translation are all welcome. Including your iOS version and the passage involved makes a report much easier to act on.
